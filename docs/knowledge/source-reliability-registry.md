# Source Reliability Registry

Reliability is use-case specific. A breeder can be authoritative for its own parentage claim and still be unsuitable evidence for yield, medical effects, or disease resistance.

## Tiers

- **A — decision source:** peer-reviewed research, university extension, government agriculture/regulation, credible batch-specific lab reports, official product/safety labels.
- **B — context-limited official source:** manufacturers, breeders, product sheets, equipment manuals, sensor/API documentation. Use for the publisher's claims and specifications, not unbiased performance proof.
- **C — anecdotal/lead source:** forums, Reddit, YouTube, Discord, consumer cultivar databases, dispensary menus and reviews. Use to discover patterns or evidence leads; cross-check before decisions.
- **D — marketing/unsupported:** SEO/affiliate pages, unattributed seed-bank copy, unsupported health claims, copied or AI-generated articles. Do not use for decision logic without independent verification.

## Initial authorities

- UC IPM, USDA/APHIS, Cornell, Penn State, NC State, Maryland, Oregon State, Colorado State, Clemson and Virginia Tech extension: Tier A for IPM, soil, fertility, water and horticultural principles.
- UC IPM Thrips and Powdery Mildew pages, reviewed 2026-08-06: Tier A for distinguishing thrips-compatible silvering/stippling/scarring and black frass from superficial white-to-gray powdery growth. These traits support an evidence differential; they do not justify a confirmed diagnosis from one ambiguous photo.
- USDA Agricultural Research Service Image Gallery, reviewed 2026-08-08: Tier A government media source for rights-cleared IPM evaluation candidates when the individual asset is marked copyright-free/public domain. Preserve the asset URL, ARS identifier, caption, photographer, downloaded original, and review date. Use the caption as a candidate label, not automatic diagnostic ground truth; independently verify the organism, visible sign, host, and intended evaluation label. Exclude any asset whose page says otherwise or whose rights are ambiguous.
- IPM Images / Bugwood catalog record in USDA Ag Data Commons, reviewed 2026-08-08: Tier A government catalog context for public-domain agriculture and pest-management images organized by taxonomy, damage type, disease, commodity, and biological control. Admit an image to GrowPath evaluation only after retaining its individual image page, creator/agency credit, identifier, rights statement, host, visible sign, and reviewer-confirmed label. The collection label does not prove that a particular photo shows the claimed diagnosis at app-ready quality.
- iNaturalist licensed observation images, reviewed 2026-08-08: Tier C candidate media for pest organism shape, life-stage, host, and field-context evaluation. Commercial GrowPath intake is restricted to the image's own CC0 or CC BY license; never rely on the observation-data license, the platform default, or a dataset-level description as the photo license. Research Grade/community agreement is not diagnostic ground truth. Preserve attribution and license metadata, and cross-check identity and visible plant damage with a Tier A source. Exclude CC BY-NC, no-derivatives, all-rights-reserved, missing-license, and ambiguous media.
- Wikimedia Commons licensed plant media, reviewed 2026-08-11: Tier C candidate-media context for Plant ID QA only. Commons states that file requirements vary and that reusers must verify each file's copyright and license details. GrowPath may admit only individually reviewed CC0 1.0 or CC BY 4.0 files to this fixture, with the file page, original URL, creator, attribution, and license link retained. Exclude share-alike, noncommercial, no-derivatives, all-rights-reserved, unclear, and incompatible files. A filename, category, caption, or Commons placement is not botanical ground truth; independently review visible morphology and cross-check the taxon against Tier A botanical sources.
- Credible COAs: Tier A only for the named lab, method, sample, batch and date. Never generalize one result to a cultivar or future batch.
- Official fertilizer/product labels: Tier A for guaranteed analysis and legal/safety label content; manufacturer pages are Tier B for instructions/specifications.
- Breeder sites: Tier B for official name, claimed parentage, seed type and breeder flowering estimate; not proof of yield, effects, resistance or future phenotype.
- Leafly, Weedmaps and menus: Tier C for market listings, consumer language and COA leads; not agronomy or cultivar identity.
- Forums/social video: Tier C anecdote. Never sole support for diagnosis, pesticide use, legal guidance or genetics provenance.
- Crime Pays But Botany Doesn't, reviewed 2026-07-25: Tier C educational and QA context for field-observation vocabulary, plant-family pattern recognition, flower/leaf morphology, ecology, and candidate discriminating questions. Do not copy or retain its videos, audio, frames, transcripts, or thumbnails without creator permission, and do not treat a host identification as GrowPath ground truth. Cross-check candidates and identification rules against Tier A botanical keys, government plant records, herbarium evidence, or university extension. Never use it as sole support for species confirmation, diagnosis, IPM treatment, toxicity, edibility, or legal status.
- USDA NRCS PLANTS Database, reviewed 2026-07-25: Tier A government context for plant taxonomy, distribution, documentation, and representative morphology. Exact identification still requires adequate visible characters and the appropriate key, specimen, herbarium, or expert confirmation within the record's scope.
- University of Minnesota Extension crop lifecycle pages, reviewed 2026-08-20: Tier A crop-planning context for sweet basil, lettuce, strawberries, and apples. Basil supports a tender-annual repeated-leaf-harvest path; lettuce requires whole-head versus repeated-leaf and succession distinctions; strawberry planning must distinguish June-bearing perennial systems from commonly annual day-neutral systems; and apple planning is long-lived, seasonal, woody, rootstock- and scion-dependent. Minnesota dates and climate guidance must not be universalized. Species names do not establish cultivar, rootstock, production system, region, first-harvest date, productive lifespan, or yield.
- Penn State and University of Minnesota Extension representative crop-path pages, reviewed 2026-08-20: Tier A crop-planning context for long-lived non-harvest pothos, annual type-dependent marigolds, short single-cut radish microgreen trays, and finite repeated-flush button-mushroom substrate cycles. These profiles distinguish plant and fungal starts and production patterns; they do not establish universal growth rates, environmental recipes, harvest dates, yields, cultivar identity, or productive lifespan for another system.
- Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew Plants of the World Online / World Checklist of Vascular Plants, reviewed 2026-07-27: Tier A expert-reviewed vascular-plant name, synonymy, distribution, trait, and bibliography context. It can support accepted-name and range checks, but it does not identify the user's specimen without adequate diagnostic characters, an appropriate key/flora, herbarium evidence, or expert review.
- Global Biodiversity Information Facility Species and Occurrence APIs, reviewed 2026-07-27: Tier A institutional taxonomy-backbone and occurrence-record context for candidate names and geographic plausibility. Individual occurrence records retain their own identification and coordinate quality; a map match is not visual species confirmation and should be cross-checked against Kew, USDA, a regional flora, herbarium evidence, or expert review.
- iNaturalist observations and community identification, reviewed 2026-08-11: Tier C for candidate occurrence, season, lookalike, cultivated-crop coverage, and community-identification leads. Research-grade wild observations and captive/cultivated casual observations must be collected and labeled separately. Needs ID, Research Grade, casual status, captive/cultivated status, computer vision, or community agreement is not automatic GrowPath ground truth. Review the specific record, evidence, taxon agreement, location precision, and photo license; never infer commercial reuse or model-training permission.
- Facebook grower posts and groups, reviewed 2026-07-25: Tier C for candidate question language, QA/evaluation leads, and photo-quality failure examples only. Meta prohibits automated collection without prior permission, and private-group access does not grant creator media rights. Do not scrape, copy, cache, or train on posts or images without platform authorization plus image-level creator permission, intended-use approval, privacy review, and de-identification. Likes, comments, captions, and group consensus are not confirmed diagnoses. Gold evaluation cases require a separately confirmed outcome and Tier A cross-check; poor media may be retained only as a rights-reviewed negative control for rejection and retake guidance.
- NC State Extension Gardener Handbook, propagation chapter: Tier A for general cutting-propagation principles including high-humidity water-loss control and clean, low-fertility, well-drained, moisture-retentive media. It cannot prove hidden roots or set a cannabis-specific completion day.
- `HORT.20250043`, _Light, Temperature, and Relative Humidity Influence the Adventitious Rooting of Cannabis Stem Cuttings_: Tier A peer-reviewed propagation context from two Korean hemp cultivars. Treat tested environment ranges as cultivar- and study-specific context because cultivar responses differed and combined-variable effects were not tested.
- USDA ARS, _Hemp Germplasm Laboratory Tissue Culture Protocol_ (2025): Tier A official hemp germplasm laboratory context for the Stage 0-4 workflow, sterile handling, contamination control, rooting, and acclimation. It is not a universal production recipe, pathogen test, or individual-batch release record.
- Holmes et al. (2021), DOI `10.3389/fpls.2021.732344`: Tier A primary drug-type cannabis tissue-culture research for genotype- and protocol-dependent media response, contamination, rooting, acclimation, and recovery context. Cross-check with the USDA ARS workflow and the owner's measured batch records; do not convert study conditions into silent defaults.
- `PMC9404914`, _Postharvest Operations of Cannabis and Their Effect on Cannabinoid Content_: Tier A peer-reviewed review for post-harvest process factors and the fact that drying time varies by method; it does not establish one universal completion day.
- `PMC10071647`, _Glandular trichome development, morphology, and maturation are influenced by plant age and genotype in high THC-containing cannabis inflorescences_, reviewed 2026-08-12: Tier A primary, CC BY 4.0 evidence for resolved gland-head morphology, clear/cloudy/brown class semantics, CIELAB translucency and red-score method context, and one annotated count example (83 clear, 96 milky, 52 brown). The annotated panel leaks the authors' labels and is not a blind recognition input. Genotype, plant age, environment, asynchronous development and stress prevent transfer to a universal harvest threshold or whole-plant percentage.
- `PMC10610221`, _Morphological Characterization of Cannabis sativa L. Throughout Its Complete Life Cycle_, reviewed 2026-08-12: Tier A primary, CC BY 4.0 evidence containing resolved transparent, opaque/milky and amber gland-head panels. Use for qualitative class and gland-head-shape QA, not exact percentage ground truth, a universal week-based harvest rule or whole-plant inference.
- `PMC10648736`, _Comprehensive Phenotypic Characterization of Diverse Drug-Type Cannabis Varieties from the Canadian Legal Market_, reviewed 2026-08-12: Tier A primary, CC BY 4.0 evidence whose Supplementary Figure S3 supplies phone-macro clear, milky and amber-plus-milky exemplars. The panels are qualitative class references only; they do not contain per-head percentages or establish universal harvest timing.
- Lorberboym et al. (2026), DOI `10.3390/agriculture16040460`, _Estimating Cannabis Flower Maturity in Greenhouse Conditions Using Computer Vision_, reviewed 2026-08-12: Tier A primary, CC BY 4.0 method evidence for sharpness-filtered 512x512 phone-macro patches, separate trichome detection, padded single-head classification and separate detection/class metrics. The paper does not publish a downloadable image dataset or trained weights. Its study-specific results are not GrowPath accuracy evidence and must not be transferred without an independently reviewed GrowPath evaluation.
- Hugging Face `siccan/tricomas-semillero-cannabis`, reviewed 2026-08-12: Tier D discovery-only quarantine. The dataset card declares CC BY 4.0 and exposes 75 images, but its README reports approximately 25% current detection and says the main limitation is the lack of a labeled real-trichome dataset. The file tree also contains `OIP` web-search assets without image-level creator provenance. Do not copy, label, train on, or score the repository as GrowPath ground truth. Reconsider only a specific original file whose creator, license, capture metadata, and labels have all been independently reviewed.
- `PMID 6643`, _The stability of cannabis and its preparations on storage_: Tier A peer-reviewed evidence that light exposure degrades cannabinoids and that cannabis material should be protected from light; use as quality-preservation evidence, not a mold or safety determination.
- Owner observation recorded 2026-07-21: Tier B user/process evidence for planning a controlled dry around 10-14 days, treating a hot, fast, low-humidity 5-7 day endpoint as a possible quality concern, and recognizing that longer than 14 days can occur but is not recommended as routine. Cross-check against measurements and post-harvest research; never convert a range into automatic completion.
- Owner observation recorded 2026-08-08: Tier B user/process evidence that a representative calyx sample near 30% amber should be surfaced as an open harvest window rather than postponed solely because some pistils remain fresh. Use this as product decision context, not a universal biological threshold: preserve sample location, effect goal, breeder timing, whole-plant maturity, uneven/new growth, and qualified macro evidence, and never infer a percentage from unresolved pixels.
- Owner observation recorded 2026-08-25: Tier B user/setup evidence that the reviewed AC Infinity QA export's `LIGHT` column represented the setup's controller lighting on/off or output state and that no illuminance detector was installed. Preserve the raw provider value and reviewed interpretation, do not convert it to lux or call it measured brightness, and do not generalize this setup-specific statement to another AC Infinity device or export without reviewing that setup and provider schema.
- Google YouTube Embedded Players / API documentation, reviewed 2026-07-22: Tier B provider documentation for supported player URL behavior, embed constraints, and provider-side data sharing. It does not prove that an individual video is available, embeddable, licensed, captioned, or appropriate for a GrowPath course.
- OpenAI Responses API and Structured Outputs documentation, reviewed 2026-08-22: Tier B provider authority for Responses request fields, image/PDF inputs, `store: false`, non-PII `safety_identifier`, and strict JSON-Schema Structured Outputs. Every runtime source entry must declare `trustedFor`, `notTrustedFor`, whether cross-checking is required, and `lastReviewedAt`. User observations are primary evidence of what the user observed, not automatic proof of cause.
